Aug. 28, 2026: The lab moves HQ to UNL


The lab has moved its headquarters to the University of Nebraska - Lincoln.

Nick has accepted as position as an Associate Professor in the School of Biological Sciences and Davidson & Fuller Chair in Life Sciences at the University of Nebraska - Lincoln. This marks the beginning of an exciting new chapter for the lab and comes with an accompanying name change: the lab will now rebrand slightly as the Physiology for Understanding and Knowldge in Ecology (PhUnKY) Lab at UNL. The primary foci of the lab will remain unchanged, but the new locations brings new opportunities for collaborations with UNL scientists and development of new project in Nebraskan and adjacent ecosystems.

Daniel Owusu Kwakye (PhD student) has transferred to UNL, where he will continue working towards his PhD. Evan Perskowski (postdoc), Snehanjana Chatterjee (PhD student), Monika Kelley (PhD student), and Isabella Beltran Triana (PhD student) will stay at TTU to finish their projects and degrees.

The lab will be looking for a new postdoc and lab manager, as well as graduate students to join at UNL. The advertisements for these will be out soon, but please fee free to reach out to Nick ahead of time if you are interested.

Aug. 7, 2026: Gwen and Clara graduate!!!


Gwen and Clara graduated with their Masters (w/ thesis) this summer! They each successfully defended strong theses, which are now available through TTU and will soon be submitted for peer-review publication.

Gwen's thesis developed a flora for Kimble County in Central Texas. This flora greatly expands what is known about the plants in the area and includes both a comprehensive species list as well as abundance data for select locations that can be revisited in future years. The flora is important, as many species are endemic to the Edward's Plateau region of Texas and can help with conservation efforts. In addition, the flora will serve future courses at the TTU Center at Junction.

Clara's thesis used an observational gradient in Central Texas to better understand the links between climate, soil, mycorrhizal colonization, and plant traits. Her results showed that patterns are clear and understandable when examined at the community scale, indicating that both plasticity and species turnover drive these relationships. The results are critical for understanding and predicting the future success of these plants in Central Texas.

Congrats Gwen and Clara!!

Jul. 28, 2026: The lab has a large showing at #ESA2026


The lab will be giving a large number of presentations at the 2026 Ecological Society of America Meeting.

On Tuesday, Evan will be discussing some very exciting initial results from our growth chamber tracer experiment that resolves mechanisms of allelopathy-induced mutualism breakdown impacts on native plant physiology. Ava will be discussing the split pot design used for that experiment and Niyomi will be discussing some of the physiology results that Evan will not be able to get to in his talk. Finally, Garrison will be showing results from his first chapter examining the temperature responses of the Cytochrome b6f complex.

On Wednesday, Monika and Dan will be presenting work from their theses. Monika will discuss field results on community-level responses to eutrophication. Dan will be showing his first chapter results on the influence (or lackthereof!) of soil nitrogen availability on temperature acclimation.

Finally, on Thursday, Gwen will be presenting her thesis work, which developed a flora for the region of the Edward's Plateau in Junction, TX. Nick will be showing new work on simulating optimal photosynthetic strategies. Come check it out!
